# Escalation Criteria

**For:** All Engineering Managers in [YOUR_SCOPE]
**Owner:** [YOUR_NAME]
**Last updated:** 2026-02-09

---

## When to Escalate

| Situation | Escalate Within |
|-----------|-----------------|
| Cross-team blocker with no owner | 48 hours |
| Date slippage on a key deliverable | Same day |
| Team health Red | Same day |
| Person at risk of leaving | Same day |
| External dependency blocking for 1+ sprint | End of sprint |
| Scope change affecting key outcomes | Before committing |

---

## How to Escalate

**Channel:** Slack DM to [YOUR_NAME]

**Format:**
```
Escalation: [one-line summary]
Impact: [what breaks if unresolved]
My recommendation: [your proposed action]
```

**Example:**
```
Escalation: Feature X blocked — [Dependent Team] hasn't started their part.
Impact: Misses Q2 target, breaks customer demo.
My recommendation: Add to [your architecture/design forum] agenda this week, align with [EM Name].
```

---

## What NOT to Escalate

- Status updates (use Jira Plan)
- Things you can resolve yourself within your team
- Questions that can wait for the next 1:1
- "FYI" items (send as Slack message, not escalation)

---

## What Happens After You Escalate

1. I acknowledge within 4 hours (business hours)
2. If I can resolve directly, I will and report back
3. If it needs broader coordination, I'll schedule or handle in the next relevant meeting
4. Outcome gets tracked in the key outcomes tracker or decision log

---

## My Commitment

- I won't shoot the messenger. Escalating early is always better than escalating late.
- If you're unsure whether to escalate, escalate. I'd rather filter noise than miss signal.
- I'll never penalize raising a concern. I will notice if you sit on something that needed to surface.
