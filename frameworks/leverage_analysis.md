# Leverage Analysis

*Based on Andy Grove's High Output Management*

Your most valuable resource is your attention. This framework helps you identify where your time creates the most value and ruthlessly focus there.

---

## The Core Equation

> **Your Output = Output of your organization + Output of neighboring organizations you influence**

This means:
- Your individual work matters far less than how you multiply others
- An hour spent enabling 10 people = 10 hours of impact
- Sometimes helping another team is higher leverage than optimizing your own
- Your leverage increases as you move up (more people to multiply)

---

## Leverage Categories

### High Leverage Activities

Activities where your time creates disproportionate value:

**Setting Direction**
- Defining strategy that guides thousands of decisions
- Creating clarity that eliminates unnecessary work
- Making one decision that unblocks many people
- Communicating vision that aligns the organization

**Building Systems**
- Creating processes that scale without you
- Building feedback loops that self-correct
- Designing organizations that produce output
- Establishing standards that guide decisions

**Developing People**
- Coaching someone who will coach others
- Giving feedback that changes behavior
- Sponsoring someone into a higher-leverage role
- Building capabilities that compound

**Removing Blockers**
- Eliminating obstacles for many people
- Making decisions others are waiting on
- Resolving conflicts that stall teams
- Clearing ambiguity that creates confusion

**Information Multipliers**
- Communicating once to inform many
- Creating documentation that answers repeat questions
- Sharing context that improves decisions across teams
- Building shared understanding

### Low Leverage Activities

Activities where your time creates limited value:

**Doing Work Others Could Do**
- Making decisions others could make
- Attending meetings where you add little
- Reviewing things that don't need your review
- Being a bottleneck for information or approvals

**Individual Output**
- Writing code yourself (when you lead many teams)
- Doing analysis that others could do
- Creating documents others could create
- Tasks that don't require your level/context

**Reactive Time**
- Constantly in meetings without strategic impact
- Firefighting problems that shouldn't reach you
- Answering questions that could be answered elsewhere
- Managing up excessively

**Negative Leverage**
- Micromanaging (demotivates and disempowers)
- Making decisions that should be delegated
- Creating processes that slow people down
- Inserting yourself unnecessarily

## Common Leverage Leaks

### "Only I Can Do This" Trap
False belief that your involvement is necessary. Reality: Others can do more than you think if given context and trust.

**Fix:** For each "only I can do this," ask: Could someone else do 80% as well with some training? If yes, delegate.

### Meeting Overflow
Calendar filled with meetings that don't require you or achieve anything.

**Fix:** Audit every recurring meeting. For each: Why am I here? What would happen if I stopped attending?

### Approval Bottleneck
You're required to approve things, creating delays and dependencies.

**Fix:** Push decisions down. Create clear criteria for when escalation is needed. Trust more.

### Information Hub
You're the only one with context, so everything flows through you.

**Fix:** Share context broadly. Create documentation. Make information accessible, not dependent on you.

### Heroics Addiction
You love being the one who saves the day.

**Fix:** Build systems that prevent fires. Celebrate prevention, not firefighting.

## Leverage by Activity Type

### Meetings

| Meeting Type | Leverage | How to Maximize |
|--------------|----------|-----------------|
| 1:1s | High | Focus on development, not status |
| Staff meetings | Medium-High | Make decisions, don't just report |
| Status meetings | Low | Can often be async |
| Decision meetings | High | Come prepared, leave with decisions |
| Information meetings | Low | Replace with written updates |
| External meetings | Varies | Be selective, prepare well |

### Communication

| Type | Leverage | How to Maximize |
|------|----------|-----------------|
| All-hands | High | Clarify strategy, answer real questions |
| Email to many | Medium-High | Well-crafted, saves repeat conversations |
| Slack | Low-Medium | Quick unblocking, but often reactive |
| Documents | High | Good docs answer questions for years |
| 1:1 convos | Medium | High for important topics, low for FYI |

### Decisions

| Type | Leverage | How to Maximize |
|------|----------|-----------------|
| Strategy decisions | High | These shape everything downstream |
| People decisions | High | Right people in right roles = multiplier |
| Process decisions | High | Good processes scale; bad ones drag |
| Technical decisions | Medium | Often should be delegated |
| Administrative | Low | Delegate or batch |

## Delegation as Leverage

### The 70/10/80 Rule

> Find someone at 70% of your capability, invest 10% teaching them, and accept 80% of your quality as success.

**Why this works:**
- 80% of your quality is often good enough
- You free up 100% of that time for higher-leverage work
- They grow, becoming more capable for next time
- Organizations scale through delegation, not through heroes

### What to Delegate

| Delegate This | Keep This |
|---------------|-----------|
| Decisions others could make | Decisions only you can make |
| Work that develops others | Work that requires your unique context |
| Recurring tasks | Novel, strategic work |
| Detailed execution | Setting direction and standards |
| Information gathering | Information synthesis and decision |

### How to Delegate Well

1. **Set clear expectations** - What does success look like?
2. **Provide context** - Why does this matter? What's the background?
3. **Define authority** - What can they decide vs. escalate?
4. **Agree on check-ins** - How will you stay informed?
5. **Get out of the way** - Let them do it their way
6. **Give feedback** - Help them improve for next time
