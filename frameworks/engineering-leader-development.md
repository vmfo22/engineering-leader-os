# Area 1: Managing Up

*Shaping how leadership sees your org*

## Why This Matters

Your manager and senior stakeholders form opinions about your org whether you shape them or not. Proactive communication builds trust, secures resources, and creates air cover for your teams.

## Reflection Questions

**Answer these honestly:**

1. When did you last proactively update your manager on your org's priorities and progress (not in a scheduled meeting)?
   >

2. Does your manager know your top 3 priorities and the trade-offs you're making?
   >

3. What's the narrative about your org at the VP/C-level? Is it accurate?
   >

4. When did you last ask your manager "What should I know that I might not be seeing?"
   >

5. Do you bring problems with proposed solutions, or just problems?
   >

## Action Items

- [ ] Build async update rhythm with your manager — weekly summary or bi-weekly 1-pager when 1:1s slip
- [ ] Create a quarterly "State of the Org" summary (1-pager)
- [ ] Ask your manager: "What's happening at your level that I should know about?"
- [ ] Frame your org's investment case in business terms, not just technical terms

# Area 3: Delegation

*Scaling through others, not doing it yourself*

## Why This Matters

Leaders who don't delegate become bottlenecks. Your job is to multiply impact through others, not to be the best individual contributor in the room.

## Reflection Questions

1. What are you holding onto that someone else could own (even if they'd do it 80% as well)?
   >

2. When you delegate, do you delegate outcomes or tasks?
   >

3. What decisions are people waiting on you for that they could make themselves?
   >

4. Who on your team is ready for more responsibility but not getting it?
   >

5. What would you stop doing if you had to cut 20% of your workload?
   >

## Action Items

- [ ] Identify 3 things you currently own that could be delegated
- [ ] For each: define the outcome, set a handoff date
- [ ] Practice "What do you think we should do?" before giving answers
- [ ] Cut 20%: stop attending meetings that don't need you

# Skills Prioritization

*After completing the 8-area assessment, use this section to prioritize which skills to develop first.*

## The Shift AI Creates for Leaders

AI compresses the execution layer. Code generation, analysis, documentation — becoming commoditized. What becomes *more* valuable: judgment, narrative, people development, speed of decision. Technical credibility (systems thinking, architecture, bridging worlds) remains a differentiator but it's table stakes now. The skills that separate you at this level are the ones your assessment flagged as red.

## Priority 1: Decisive Action Under Uncertainty

**Why first:** Delayed decisions compound. Team restructuring, strategic direction, performance conversations — every day of delay has downstream cost. AI accelerates cycle times, which means delayed decisions compound faster.

**In practice:**
- Make decisions at 80% confidence — 80% is enough for reversible decisions
- Set decision deadlines for yourself (not just delivery deadlines)
- When gathering more input, ask: "Will more information actually change my decision?"

**Unlocks:** Strategic Thinking Time (Area 5) and Delegation (Area 3). Can't delegate what you haven't decided.

## Priority 2: Strategic Communication and Narrative Building

**Why second:** In an AI world where execution gets cheaper, the leader who shapes the narrative wins resources, alignment, and air cover. Good content when it happens isn't enough — it needs to be systematic.

**In practice:**
- Weekly async update to your manager (even 3 bullets in Slack)
- Share leadership-level framing *down* to teams: "Here's why what you're doing matters"
- Build a quarterly "State of the Org" 1-pager
- Frame investment cases as business cases, not technical alarms

**Addresses:** Managing Up (Area 1), Celebrating Wins (Area 8), Peer Relationships (Area 7). One skill, three areas.

## Priority 3: Developing Leaders for AI-Augmented Teams

**Why third:** Highest-leverage activity — growing EMs from team-leads into org-leaders. AI makes this *more* critical: as execution speeds up, you need leaders who make judgment calls, set direction, and adapt without waiting for you.

**In practice:**
- 5 min development check at end of every EM 1:1 (not status — growth only)
- One piece of developmental feedback per EM per month
- Push EMs to use AI tools themselves — model the behavior, don't mandate it
- Ask: "What decision did you make this week that you would have escalated to me before?"
- Identify successor candidate

**Addresses:** Developing Leaders (Area 4). Also reduces operational load as EMs take on more.

## Priority 4: Scope Expansion

**Why fourth:** Stop being defined by your current scope. AI blurs boundaries between traditional domains. Not a skill you learn in a course — a positioning play through relationships and strategic bets.

**In practice:**
- Deepen relationships at the intersection of your domain and adjacent ones
- Own a cross-cutting narrative that spans value streams
- Be the first leader with a credible AI adoption story for engineering teams
- Find a mentor/coach outside the company

**Addresses:** Career Development (Area 2), Peer Relationships (Area 7). The 6-12 month play.

## What NOT to Focus On

- **More technical depth** — you have enough. AI fills remaining gaps. Don't hide in technical work.
- **Process optimization** — tempting (a common leader trap), but operational, not strategic.
- **All 8 areas simultaneously** — the 4 priorities above cascade into all 8.

## The Cascade

```
Decisive Action (P1)
  → Unblocks Strategic Thinking Time
  → Unblocks Delegation (can't delegate undecided things)

Strategic Communication (P2)
  → Fixes Managing Up
  → Enables Celebrating Wins (share impact down)
  → Builds Peer Relationships (visibility)

Developing Leaders (P3)
  → Directly addresses the gap
  → Reduces operational load (cascades to Strategic Thinking)

Scope Expansion (P4)
  → Career Development
  → Peer Relationships beyond current circles
```

Four skills. Eight areas addressed. The order matters — Priority 1 unlocks everything downstream.
