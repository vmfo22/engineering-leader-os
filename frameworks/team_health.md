# Team Health Framework

*Synthesized from Patrick Lencioni, Paloma Medina, and Lara Hogan*

Healthy teams outperform unhealthy teams. This framework helps you assess, monitor, and improve team health across your organization.

---

## The Five Dysfunctions (Lencioni)

Patrick Lencioni's model shows how team problems stack:

```
                    Inattention to
                       RESULTS
                         ↑
                   Avoidance of
                  ACCOUNTABILITY
                         ↑
                   Lack of
                   COMMITMENT
                         ↑
                  Fear of
                   CONFLICT
                         ↑
                  Absence of
                    TRUST
```

**The insight:** You can't fix higher levels without fixing lower levels first. A team that avoids conflict can't truly commit. A team without trust can't have honest conflict.

### Level 1: Trust

**What it looks like:**
- People admit mistakes and weaknesses
- People ask for help
- People give each other benefit of the doubt
- People don't play politics or protect turf
- People are vulnerable with each other

**Warning signs of broken trust:**
- People hide mistakes
- People don't share concerns
- Meetings feel performative
- Back-channel conversations instead of direct ones
- Blame culture

**How to build trust:**
- Model vulnerability yourself
- Share your mistakes and learnings
- Create psychological safety (reward honesty, don't punish)
- Do team-building that creates genuine connection
- Address trust-breaking behavior directly

### Level 2: Conflict

**What healthy conflict looks like:**
- Passionate debates about ideas
- People disagree openly, not privately
- Conflict focuses on concepts, not personalities
- People say what they think in the room
- Conflict resolves to decisions

**Warning signs of unhealthy conflict patterns:**
- Artificial harmony (no one disagrees)
- Discussions that go in circles
- People agree in meetings, disagree afterward
- Personal attacks or grudges
- Avoidance of difficult topics

**How to enable healthy conflict:**
- Explicitly permission conflict
- Mine for conflict (ask: "What are we not saying?")
- Praise people who raise difficult topics
- Demonstrate that disagreement is safe
- Keep it about ideas, redirect when it gets personal

### Level 3: Commitment

**What commitment looks like:**
- Decisions are clear
- Everyone supports decisions, even if they disagreed
- People align even without consensus
- Timelines and outcomes are understood
- No second-guessing once decided

**Warning signs of weak commitment:**
- Decisions get revisited repeatedly
- People say "they decided" not "we decided"
- Unclear who owns what
- Ambiguity used as an excuse
- Passive resistance to decisions

**How to build commitment:**
- Ensure everyone is heard before deciding
- Make decisions explicit ("We've decided X")
- Disagree and commit—model it yourself
- Close debates with clear resolution
- Revisit decisions only with new information

### Level 4: Accountability

**What accountability looks like:**
- Team members call each other out
- Poor performance is addressed
- Peer pressure to deliver
- High standards are maintained
- People follow through on commitments

**Warning signs of weak accountability:**
- Leader has to be the bad guy
- Missed commitments aren't addressed
- People let teammates slide
- Standards erode over time
- Resentment toward low performers

**How to build accountability:**
- Clarify standards and expectations
- Make commitments public
- Regularly review progress against commitments
- Create peer accountability, not just leader accountability
- Address issues early and directly

### Level 5: Results

**What results focus looks like:**
- Team goals above individual goals
- Shared definition of success
- Celebrating team wins, not just individual wins
- Willingness to sacrifice for team outcomes
- Ego doesn't drive decisions

**Warning signs of weak results focus:**
- People optimize their area at team expense
- Status and ego drive behavior
- People aren't sure what team success means
- No one owns overall outcomes
- Finger-pointing when things fail

**How to build results focus:**
- Define success for the team, not just individuals
- Reward team outcomes
- Make results visible
- Share success and failure as a team
- Tie individual recognition to team impact

## Resilience and Core Needs

### Team Needs

Every team member needs:

**Belonging:** Do I fit here? Am I valued?
**Improvement/Progress:** Am I getting better? Are we making progress?
**Choice:** Do I have agency? Can I influence my work?
**Equality/Fairness:** Am I treated fairly? Are decisions equitable?
**Predictability:** Do I know what's expected? Can I anticipate change?
**Significance:** Does my work matter? Am I recognized?

**When a need is threatened, people react.** Understanding which need is at risk helps you respond appropriately.

### BICEPS Check

When someone is upset, struggling, or reactive, ask:
- Is their **Belonging** threatened?
- Is their sense of **Improvement** threatened?
- Is their **Choice** threatened?
- Is their sense of **Equality** threatened?
- Is their **Predictability** threatened?
- Is their **Significance** threatened?

### Resilience During Change

Change threatens needs. During transitions:

1. Acknowledge what's being lost
2. Be honest about uncertainty
3. Give people whatever choices you can
4. Communicate frequently and transparently
5. Create stability where possible
6. Celebrate progress to feed improvement need

## Warning Signs to Watch

### Individual Level
- Sudden disengagement
- Unusual conflict or frustration
- Dropping quality of work
- Withdrawal from team activities
- Unusual absences

### Team Level
- Increasing blame
- Decisions not sticking
- Meetings feel unproductive
- Declining velocity without cause
- Rising attrition interest

### Organizational Level
- Silos strengthening
- Collaboration declining
- Culture shifts negatively
- Trust in leadership eroding
- Best people leaving

## Intervention Playbook

### When Trust Is Broken

**Quick wins:**
- Have honest 1:1 conversations
- Model vulnerability yourself
- Address specific trust-breaking behavior
- Create team time for connection

**Deeper work:**
- Facilitate structured trust-building exercises
- Bring in outside help if needed
- Address root causes (bad actors, systemic issues)
- Be patient—trust builds slowly

### When Conflict Is Unhealthy

**Quick wins:**
- Explicitly name the conflict style problem
- Create structured debate formats
- Praise people who raise difficult topics
- Redirect personal attacks immediately

**Deeper work:**
- Train on healthy conflict skills
- Examine what makes conflict feel unsafe
- Model productive disagreement
- Coach individuals on conflict skills

### When Commitment Wavers

**Quick wins:**
- Make decisions explicit and documented
- Clearly assign ownership
- Review and confirm alignment
- Ask "what would make you not support this?"

**Deeper work:**
- Ensure voices are heard before decisions
- Examine decision-making process
- Build disagree-and-commit muscle
- Increase transparency around decisions

### When Accountability Slips

**Quick wins:**
- Clarify expectations explicitly
- Make commitments visible
- Regular progress reviews
- Address issues early

**Deeper work:**
- Build peer accountability culture
- Examine why people don't hold each other accountable
- Train on giving difficult feedback
- Model accountability yourself

### When Results Suffer

**Quick wins:**
- Clarify team goals
- Make results visible
- Celebrate team wins
- Tie recognition to team outcomes

**Deeper work:**
- Examine incentives (are they aligned?)
- Restructure if needed
- Address individual performance issues
- Reset culture around outcomes

## Monthly Health Rhythm

1. Check in with each manager on their team's health
2. Note any warning signs
3. Identify teams needing attention
4. Plan interventions if needed
5. Track trends over time
