# Developing Engineering Managers: A Director's Framework

This framework provides practical tools for growing your EMs from capable managers into strategic leaders who can operate with increasing autonomy.

---

## 1. Assessing EM Readiness and Growth Edges

### The EM Capability Matrix

Assess each EM across six dimensions. Rate 1-5 where 1 = needs significant development, 5 = could coach others.

| Dimension | What to Look For |
|-----------|------------------|
| **Technical Leadership** | Can they make sound architectural decisions? Do engineers respect their technical judgment? Can they translate business needs into technical direction? |
| **People Development** | Are their people growing? Do they give hard feedback? Can they coach someone out gracefully? |
| **Delivery Excellence** | Do they hit commitments? Can they scope and de-risk? Do they escalate appropriately? |
| **Strategic Thinking** | Do they connect work to business outcomes? Can they prioritize ruthlessly? Do they anticipate vs. react? |
| **Stakeholder Management** | Can they manage up and across? Do they build trust with product/design? Can they say no diplomatically? |
| **Organizational Influence** | Do they improve things beyond their team? Are they sought out for input? Can they drive change? |

### Growth Edge Identification Questions

Ask yourself about each EM:

- What would break if they were promoted tomorrow?
- What do I still do for them that they should own?
- What feedback have I been avoiding giving them?
- Where do they default to asking permission vs. acting?
- What's the pattern in the problems they escalate?

### The "Shadow Promotion" Test

Imagine promoting each EM to your role for one month. Write down:
1. The three things that would go well
2. The three things that would fail
3. What they'd need to learn first

The gaps you identify are their growth edges.

## 2. Delegation Frameworks

### Delegation Levels (The Ladder)

Use explicit language about which level you're delegating at:

| Level | What You Say | When to Use |
|-------|--------------|-------------|
| **L1: Execute** | "Do exactly this, report back" | Crisis, new EM, high-stakes |
| **L2: Research** | "Investigate and recommend, I'll decide" | Building their judgment |
| **L3: Recommend** | "Decide and tell me before acting" | Growing trust |
| **L4: Act & Inform** | "Decide and act, then let me know" | Proven judgment |
| **L5: Own** | "Own this completely, I trust you" | Ready for stretch |

**Key practice:** Tell them which level they're at. "I'm delegating this at L3 — come back with a recommendation and we'll discuss before you move."

### RAPID for Decision Rights

RAPID® is a decision-rights framework from Bain & Company. Cleaner than RACI for engineering organizations:

| Role | Meaning | Notes |
|------|---------|-------|
| **R** - Recommend | Proposes the decision | Usually the EM |
| **A** - Agree | Must agree (has veto) | Use sparingly — one or two people |
| **P** - Perform | Executes once decided | The team |
| **I** - Input | Consulted before decision | Stakeholders |
| **D** - Decide | Makes the call | Often the EM; you only when needed |

**Anti-pattern to watch:** If you're always the D, you're not developing them.

### The Delegation Diagnostic

For each major area you own, ask:
1. Could an EM own this? (If no, why not?)
2. Which EM is closest to ready?
3. What would they need to own it?
4. What's my timeline to hand it off?

### Outcome vs. Task Delegation

| Task Delegation | Outcome Delegation |
|-----------------|-------------------|
| "Set up weekly syncs with Platform team" | "Improve our relationship with Platform team so we're unblocked within 24 hours" |
| "Hire two senior engineers" | "Get the team to a state where we can ship the Q2 roadmap" |
| "Write a post-mortem" | "Make sure this class of incident doesn't happen again" |

**The test:** If you're specifying the how, you're delegating tasks. If you're specifying the what, you're delegating outcomes.

## 3. Stretch Assignments

### Types of Stretch for EMs

| Stretch Type | Examples | Develops |
|--------------|----------|----------|
| **Scope expansion** | Own an additional team temporarily, lead a cross-team initiative | Scale, prioritization |
| **Stakeholder complexity** | Lead exec review, own relationship with difficult partner team | Influence, communication |
| **Ambiguity** | Define strategy for new area, figure out why velocity dropped | Strategic thinking |
| **Visibility** | Present to leadership, represent org in hiring, lead all-hands | Executive presence |
| **Hard people situations** | Coach a struggling performer, navigate reorg, handle conflict | People leadership |
| **Technical depth** | Own architectural decision, lead technical strategy | Technical judgment |

### Designing a Stretch Assignment

**Before assigning:**
1. What specific capability will this develop?
2. What does success look like?
3. What support will they need?
4. What's the blast radius if they fail?
5. How will I create space for them to struggle productively?

**The 70/30 rule:** A good stretch is 70% within their capability and 30% beyond it. If it's 50/50 or worse, they need more support or a smaller stretch.

### Stretch Assignment Conversation Template

"I want to give you [assignment] because I think it will help you grow in [specific area]. Here's what success looks like: [outcome]. I'm delegating this at Level [X]. I'll support you by [specific support]. What questions do you have? What concerns?"

### Common Stretch Assignments for EMs Ready for Director Track

- Lead a cross-functional initiative with no direct authority
- Own hiring strategy for your area (not just execution)
- Represent the org in exec-level planning
- Navigate a reorg or team split
- Turn around a struggling team
- Build a business case for new investment
- Mentor a newer EM

## 4. Developmental Feedback Approaches

### Operational vs. Developmental Feedback

| Operational | Developmental |
|-------------|---------------|
| "The sprint review could have been shorter" | "I notice you tend to over-explain. What's driving that?" |
| "You should have escalated that earlier" | "What's your mental model for when to escalate? Let's calibrate." |
| "The hiring process took too long" | "You're risk-averse in hiring. What would help you move faster?" |

### The SBI-I Framework (Situation-Behavior-Impact-Intent)

1. **Situation:** Specific context
2. **Behavior:** What they did (observable)
3. **Impact:** What resulted
4. **Intent:** What you believe they were trying to do (generous interpretation)
5. **Question:** Open the conversation

**Example:** "In the exec review yesterday (S), you gave a lot of technical detail before the business context (B). The execs started checking phones and we ran out of time for the decision we needed (I). I think you wanted to be thorough and credible (Intent). What was your read on how it went?"

### Feedforward vs. Feedback

Instead of only discussing past behavior, discuss future behavior:

**Feedback:** "Your 1:1s with Sarah have been too tactical."
**Feedforward:** "In your next 1:1 with Sarah, try spending the first 15 minutes only on her growth. What would you ask her?"

### The Growth Edge Conversation

Have this quarterly with each EM:

1. "What do you think your growth edge is right now?"
2. "Here's what I see as your growth edge: [specific]"
3. "What would it look like if you were excellent at this?"
4. "What's getting in the way?"
5. "What's one experiment you could run this month?"
6. "How can I help?"

### Feedback Frequency Rule

If you haven't given developmental feedback to an EM in two weeks, you're probably avoiding something. Check your notes.

## 5. 1:1 Structures for Development

### The Status Trap

Signs your 1:1s are too operational:
- You spend most of the time on updates you could read in Slack
- They bring a list of small decisions for you to make
- You leave the meeting without learning anything about their growth
- The conversation would be the same with anyone in their role

### Developmental 1:1 Structure (45-60 min)

| Segment | Time | Focus |
|---------|------|-------|
| **Check-in** | 5 min | Energy, anything urgent |
| **Their agenda** | 15 min | What they want to discuss (push for non-operational) |
| **Development focus** | 15 min | One growth area, deep dive |
| **Strategic topic** | 10 min | Something beyond their current scope |
| **Commitments** | 5 min | What each of you will do |

### Questions That Drive Development

**On their growth:**
- What's been hard this week? What made it hard?
- Where did you feel out of your depth?
- What would you do differently if you could replay that?
- What's a decision you made that you're not sure about?

**On their team:**
- Who on your team is ready for more?
- Who are you avoiding having a hard conversation with?
- If you had to cut one person, who would it be? Why?
- What's the biggest people risk on your team?

**On strategy:**
- What would you do if you had my job for a week?
- What are we not talking about that we should be?
- What's going to bite us in six months?
- What would you change about how we operate?

**On their readiness:**
- What would need to be true for you to take on [bigger scope]?
- What do you think I should delegate to you that I haven't?
- If I were out for a month, what would you be worried about handling?

### The 1:1 Reset Conversation

If your 1:1s have become too operational, have this explicit conversation:

"I want to shift how we use our 1:1s. I've realized we spend too much time on status and not enough on your development and strategic thinking. Going forward, I want to spend at least half our time on [growth areas, strategic topics, longer-term thinking]. What would make that valuable for you?"

## 6. Succession Planning

### The Succession Question

Answer honestly: If you were promoted or left tomorrow, who would take your role?

| Answer | What It Means |
|--------|---------------|
| "Nobody is ready" | You haven't developed anyone — this is a gap |
| "Maybe [name] in 2 years" | Good, you have a development plan to execute |
| "[Name] is ready now" | Either promote them or they'll leave |

### Readiness Assessment Criteria

Score potential successors on:

| Criteria | Questions to Ask |
|----------|------------------|
| **Strategic capability** | Can they set direction, not just execute? Do they think in quarters and years, not just sprints? |
| **Leadership breadth** | Have they led leaders? Can they manage managers they didn't hire? |
| **Organizational influence** | Do peers and senior leaders seek their input? Can they drive change horizontally? |
| **Executive presence** | Can they represent the org to senior leadership? Do they communicate at the right altitude? |
| **Resilience** | How do they handle ambiguity, pressure, and failure? |
| **Judgment** | Do they make good calls with incomplete information? Do they know what they don't know? |

### The Development Plan for Successors

For each potential successor:

1. **Current state:** Where are they now on each readiness criterion?
2. **Gap:** What's the biggest gap between current state and readiness?
3. **Experience needed:** What experiences would close that gap?
4. **Timeline:** What's realistic?
5. **Your role:** What do you need to do to make this happen?

### Making Yourself Replaceable

Actions that develop successors:
- Bring them to meetings they wouldn't normally attend
- Have them present work you would normally present
- Give them your problems before you solve them
- Let them make decisions you would have made
- Debrief your thinking process, not just your conclusions

## 7. Scaling Through EMs

### The Director Mindset Shift

| Managing ICs | Managing Managers |
|--------------|-------------------|
| Your job is to make decisions | Your job is to develop decision-makers |
| Success = your team ships | Success = your teams ship without you |
| You solve problems | You build people who solve problems |
| You're in the room | You prepare them for the room |

### Common Director Traps

| Trap | Signs | Antidote |
|------|-------|----------|
| **The Hero** | You swoop in on every crisis, EMs wait for you | Let them struggle, coach after |
| **The Bottleneck** | Every decision needs your input | Explicit delegation levels, push decisions down |
| **The Peer** | You're still doing EM work, not Director work | Audit your calendar — what should they own? |
| **The Absentee** | You're so hands-off they feel abandoned | Regular 1:1s with depth, be available for hard stuff |
| **The Perfectionist** | You redo their work or give excessive notes | Accept 80% as good enough, coach on the 20% |

### Signs Your EMs Are Growing

Positive indicators:
- They make decisions without asking you
- They escalate less, and when they do, it's the right stuff
- They start pushing back on you
- Their teams perform when they're on vacation
- Other teams want to hire them or work with them
- They ask for more scope
- They give you feedback

Warning signs:
- They ask permission for things they should own
- Their teams depend on them for everything
- They can't articulate their strategy
- They avoid hard conversations
- They're not developing their own people

### Your Weekly Audit

Ask yourself each week:
1. What did I do this week that an EM should have done?
2. What decision did I make that an EM should have made?
3. What meeting did I attend that an EM should have attended?
4. Which EM got developmental feedback from me?
5. Which EM got a new stretch or responsibility?

## Quick Reference: Monthly EM Development Checklist

For each EM, review monthly:

- [ ] What's their current growth edge? (Capability Matrix)
- [ ] When did I last give them developmental (not operational) feedback?
- [ ] What level of delegation are they at for their key responsibilities?
- [ ] What stretch assignment are they working on?
- [ ] How developmental were our 1:1s this month?
- [ ] Are they ready for more scope? What's blocking that?
- [ ] If I were out for a month, what would break for them?

---

## Key Questions for Your Next EM 1:1s

Pick one or two to use this week:

1. "What's the hardest thing about your job right now?"
2. "Where do you feel like you're operating outside your comfort zone?"
3. "What would you do with my job for a day?"
4. "What feedback are you avoiding giving someone on your team?"
5. "What decision are you sitting on that you should just make?"
6. "What do you wish I would delegate to you?"
7. "What's something you've learned about yourself as a leader recently?"
