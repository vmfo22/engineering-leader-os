## Purpose of Skip Levels

Skip levels serve multiple purposes:
1. Build relationship with people 2+ levels down
2. Get unfiltered perspective on the organization
3. Understand how your directs are doing as managers
4. Surface issues that might not bubble up
5. Provide visibility and access to senior leadership

**Key reminder:** This is not about going around their manager. Be careful not to undermine your direct report.

## Topics to Cover

### Opening
- How are things going? (Open-ended)
- What are you working on that you're excited about?

### Their Experience
- What's going well for you?
- What's frustrating?
- What would make your job easier?

### Their Manager (Your Direct Report)
*Ask carefully—not to undermine, but to get signal:*
- How's working with [Manager Name]?
- Are you getting what you need from them?
- How could they support you better?

### The Team
- How's the team morale?
- Are there any tensions or issues?
- Who's doing great work that I should know about?

### The Organization
- Do you understand our priorities and why?
- Is there anything confusing about direction?
- What could leadership do better?

### Career
- How's your career development going?
- What would you like to be doing that you're not?
- Any opportunities you're interested in?

### Open
- What questions do you have for me?
- Is there anything else you wanted to discuss?
- Anything I should know that I probably don't?

## What to Listen For

**Signals about their manager:**
- Are they getting enough support?
- Is feedback flowing?
- Are they growing?

**Signals about the team:**
- Trust and psychological safety
- Clarity of purpose
- Team dynamics

**Signals about the organization:**
- Strategic clarity
- Communication effectiveness
- Cultural health

---

## Ground Rules (Mental Reminders)

- Listen more than talk
- Don't make promises you can't keep
- Don't solve problems that their manager should solve
- Don't share things that aren't yours to share
- If they raise a serious concern, clarify how you'll handle it
- Thank them for their candor

## Skip Level Best Practices

**Do:**
- Create psychological safety
- Be genuinely curious
- Share context they might not have
- Remember details for next time
- Follow up on things you said you would

**Don't:**
- Undermine their manager
- Make promises their manager should make
- Solve problems for them
- Share confidential information
- Forget to have skip levels (schedule them)
